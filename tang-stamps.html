Section 02

Tang stamps & dating

The tang — the flat, unsharpened base of the blade near the pivot — carries Case's manufacturer stamp. The wording and dot pattern of that stamp changed over time, which is the main way collectors narrow down when a knife was made.

Stamp eras

folding knives — year keys verified where shown
1905 – 1914
W.R. CASE & SONS CUTLERY CO. BRADFORD, PA.

Before the XX mark

The earliest Case stamps predate the double-X entirely and read out the full company name and town. No dot or number system yet — dating this early relies on the exact wording, blade steel, and handle construction.

1914 – 1920
CASE XX TESTED

The XX mark debuts

The double-X appears for the first time, inside an oval "Case XX Tested" stamp — originally a reference to a tempering/testing process, not a date code.

1920 – 1940
CASE TESTED XX

Wording variants, still no dates

Several wordings coexisted through this stretch — "Case XX," "Case Tested XX," "Case's Tested XX," and script-style "Case" stamps among them. None carry a date code; narrowing this span means comparing exact wording and stamp shape against a reference.

1940 – 1964
CASE XX

A long, stable stretch

A plain "Case XX" stamp that held for roughly two and a half decades. "Case XX Stainless" runs alongside it from 1950 on, marking the stainless-steel blade lines specifically.

1965 – 1969
CASE XX U.S.A.

"U.S.A." is added

"U.S.A." joins the stamp for the first time. Still no dots or date code — this is the last purely wording-based window before Case starts marking individual years.

1970 – 1979
CASE XX U.S.A. ••••••••••

The dot system begins — dots count down

Case starts marking the manufacture year with a row of dots beneath "U.S.A." The count runs backwards through the decade: ten dots for 1970, counting down one dot per year to a single dot for 1979.

Dots move between XX and USA

Same countdown logic, new position: the dots move to sit between the stylized "CaseXX" logo and "USA." Ten dots opens the decade in 1980, counting down to one dot in 1989. A stainless variant of the same era reads "CaseXX ··SS·· USA."

1990 – 1993
CASE XX BRADFORD, PA. 19[USA]90

The year is spelled out directly

For four years, Case drops dots entirely and stamps the two-digit year split around "USA" — "19," then "USA," then the last two digits, so a 1992 knife reads "19 USA 92." No counting required for this short window, just reading the digits.

1993 – 1999
CASE XX USA •••••••

Dots return, shorter countdown

Dots come back beneath a restyled "Case XX USA" mark, again counting down — but this cycle only runs seven years, so it opens at seven dots in 1993 rather than ten.

2000 – present
CaseXX ˣ ˣ

Small "x" marks replace dots

From 2000 on, Case switched from round dots to small "x" marks flanking the stylized "CaseXX" logo, still stepping down across each decade (a fresh countdown starts in 2000, again in 2010, and again in 2020). The exact mark-count-to-year key for this era is fine enough that it's easy to misread from a photo — cross-check a specific knife against the Case Collectors Club's chart (linked below) rather than relying on a guess.
