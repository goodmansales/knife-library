Section 02

Tang stamps & dating

The tang — the flat, unsharpened base of the blade near the pivot — carries Case's manufacturer stamp. The wording and dot pattern of that stamp changed over time, which is the main way collectors narrow down when a knife was made.

Stamp eras

general timeline — see note below
Pre-1920
CASE BROS

Early wording, no dots

The earliest stamps predate the "XX" mark entirely and are identified by wording alone — variations like "Case Bros" or early "W.R. Case" stampings. No dot system yet; dating relies on wording, blade steel, and handle construction.

1920s – 1965
CASE XX

The XX era begins

The double-X mark appears, originally referencing a tempering process. Through this long stretch, stamps read variations of "Case XX" and later "Case XX USA" — still no dots. Dating within this span leans on the exact wording and stamp shape rather than a year-by-year code.

1970 – 1979
CASE XX ⠂⠂⠂

Dots above the stamp

Case introduces dots above the "XX" to mark the manufacture year — one dot for 1970, counting upward toward ten dots for 1979. This is the start of the dot-dating system collectors rely on today.

1980 – 1989
⠂⠂⠂ CASE XX

Dots move below the stamp

The same counting system continues, but the dots relocate beneath the "XX" — one dot for 1980, building back up to ten for 1989 — so their position, not just their count, matters when dating a knife from this window.

1990 – 1999
CASE XX ⠐⠐⠐

Dots return above, system resets

Dots move back above the stamp for a third decade-long cycle. As with the two previous decades, dots are removed one at a time as the decade progresses, so a knife with fewer dots was made later in the decade.

2000s – present
CASE 10

Numeric year stamp

Case moves away from dots and begins stamping the last two digits of the actual year directly on the tang, alongside "CASE" — a much more direct system that continues today.

A note on precision: this timeline is a starting framework, not a substitute for a full dot-by-dot chart. The exact dot count for a single year, and known exceptions or transition-year overlaps, will be filled in here as the archive grows — cross-check a specific knife against a detailed chart before relying on this for a valuation or sale.
